# Orangy Blues: why each rule exists

## Source

A 69 s 9:16 talking-head clip, already cut and trimmed by the presenter ("do not touch the sequence"). Two reference
reels set the text style (kinetic words behind the head); the card compositions came from carousel and floating-card
references shared by the presenter. The edit was built over seven storyboard/render rounds; the rules below are the
decisions that survived.

## Decisions (in order) and the reason

- **Overlay only, never re-cut.** The presenter had already trimmed the sequence; any cut would break the pace he chose.
- **Orange + blue, not yellow.** Same kinetic-words engine, but a long clip needs a warmer, more varied palette; blue
  carries glow, rings and borders so orange stays the hero.
- **Visualise every sentence.** On a 60+ s clip, words alone get monotonous; one small analogy per idea keeps the eye
  moving without a second focal point.
- **Break pattern.** "Don't make every section the same grid": a different card composition per section.
- **Safe area.** Social apps overlay a right-hand rail and bottom caption area. Nothing in the top 168 px, nothing at
  x > ~940, bottom elements end by ~1600 px. Columns moved to the LEFT edge.
- **Full-screen B-roll, not cards.** Phone footage and AI stills are stronger full screen with a reveal than as small
  cards; the words move to the front layer under them.
- **Logos, not screenshots.** Tool replaced by another: show only the official logos (a flat dock with cross badges).
- **Real logo for the tool being installed.** The generic icon looked fake; use the real mark supplied by the student.
- **Transitions: two only.** The pack clips are strong; more than two cheapens it. A clip that renders a full white frame is dropped.
- **Music.** Two library tracks were rejected ("not right", "not the vibe") before the presenter named the trailer
  track he already uses in his YouTube trailers. Lesson: ask which track family he uses, match the HIT/DROP to the
  story, and change the track rather than only the volume.
- **Results scene.** Cards that were too small in a grid, then an orbit, then a blurred full-scene results outro with
  a title and slides entering one by one with margins. The presenter's call: results are the proof, so they get the
  whole frame.
- **Orbit tidy rule.** Cards in an orbit sit on one ring, fixed centre, no vertical wobble, tilt kept below ~26 degrees.
- **Check every frame.** Overlaps seen in a render mean stop, fix, re-storyboard, then render again.
- **Native-resolution crops.** A 388 px-wide copy blown up to 860 px looked soft; cropping from the original recording
  fixed it.

## What is generic and what is per video

Generic: layer order, geometry, type, palette, safe area, module catalogue, music alignment method, results outro.
Per video: the script and words, which words are big, the analogy shapes, the grid contents and order, the B-roll,
the track and its hit/drop times, and the student's own results screenshots.
